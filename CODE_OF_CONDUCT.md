# Tracelet Code of Conduct

## Purpose

Tracelet welcomes people who participate in good faith, regardless of experience, background, identity or role. We want Issues, Discussions, pull requests, reviews and other project spaces to remain useful, safe and respectful.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- Discuss ideas and code without attacking the person who proposed them.
- Give specific, constructive feedback and accept correction with professionalism.
- Respect different levels of experience, communication styles and language fluency.
- Protect private information and use synthetic examples when discussing analytics data.
- Help de-escalate misunderstandings and focus on what benefits the project and its users.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not accepted:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking or sustained unwanted contact.
- Discriminatory, degrading or sexualized language, imagery or attention.
- Personal attacks, deliberate disruption, trolling or repeated bad-faith argument.
- Publishing private information, credentials, production payloads or personal data without clear permission.
- Retaliating against someone who reports a concern or participates in an investigation.
- Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider unsafe or seriously unprofessional in a technical community.

Good-faith technical disagreement, clearly explained moderation decisions and proportionate security research are not violations by themselves.

## Scope

This policy applies to all Tracelet-managed spaces, including the repository, Issues, Discussions, pull requests, code reviews and release conversations. It also applies when someone is formally representing Tracelet in another public or private space.

## Reporting

Do not open a public Issue or Discussion to report a conduct incident. Contact the repository owner privately using the email address published on the owner's GitHub profile. Include only the information needed to understand the incident, such as links, dates and a factual description; do not forward unrelated private data.

Use GitHub's built-in content reporting and blocking tools when immediate platform action is appropriate. Security vulnerabilities are handled separately through [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md) and GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to the people needed to review and resolve the report. When practical, a maintainer who is directly involved should ask an uninvolved trusted reviewer or GitHub Support to help decide the response.

## Enforcement

Depending on context, severity and prior behavior, maintainers may:

1. Clarify expectations or request a correction.
2. Remove or edit harmful content and issue a formal warning.
3. Temporarily restrict participation in project spaces.
4. Permanently remove access or report the behavior to GitHub or the appropriate authorities.

Actions should be proportionate, documented privately where appropriate and focused on community safety rather than punishment. Retaliation or repeated violations may lead to a stronger response.
